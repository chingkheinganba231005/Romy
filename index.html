The Chapters

Chapter 1

Rediscovering Kepler's Laws

Prague, 1601. Turn twenty years of Tycho Brahe's handwritten numbers into a model of the sky.

raw telescope dataNASA/JPL custom transformersGroupKFoldGridSearchCVLinearRegression

Clean 940 naked-eye declinations of Mars written in Latin, fixing the calendars and hours along the way. Triangulate Mars's orbit the way Kepler did, and fit four rival world-models with cross-validation. Then find all three laws in the data.

On unseen observing seasons, the old circle-based model misses by 8.3′, Kepler's "eight arcminutes". The ellipse fitted to Tycho's data gives Mars an eccentricity of 0.0938 (modern value: 0.0934).

Chapter 2

Weighing the Black Hole at the Center of the Milky Way

25 years of infrared images of stars whirling around something invisible. How heavy is it?

raw telescope data stratified samplingphysics-based estimatorbootstrapforecasting

Measure accelerations that all point to one empty spot and fall off as 1/r². Fit a 13-parameter Kepler orbit to positions and Doppler velocities of the star S2. Then beat polynomial baselines at forecasting, and put error bars on the answer with the bootstrap.

Sagittarius A* weighs 4.5 ± 0.2 million suns, at 8.5 ± 0.2 kpc. The model, trained on data up to 2016, predicts S2's next pericenter passage at 2018.41. It was observed at 2018.38.

Chapter 3

Rediscovering Neptune

Paris, 1845. Uranus refuses to follow its tables. Find the planet nobody has seen.

NASA/JPL Horizons gravity simulatorresidual analysisPipeline + GridSearchCVfeature engineering

Wrap a Newtonian N-body integrator as a Scikit-Learn estimator, and read the anomaly in its residuals. Then search 480 candidate planets with a linearized pipeline and a grid search, and point the telescope on 23 September 1846.

Planet X comes out at 30 AU with 16 Earth masses (Neptune has 17). The predicted position lands 1.4° from where Neptune really was the night it was discovered.

Chapter 4

Discovering Dark Energy

1998. Everyone expects the expansion of the Universe to be slowing down. The supernovae disagree.

real telescope data StratifiedGroupKFoldfeature engineeringgeneralized least squaresgroup bootstrap

Rediscover Hubble's law, build a standard candle from light-curve stretch and colour, climb the distance ladder from Cepheids to supernovae, and compare five universes with cross-validation and a grid scan of Ωm and ΩΛ.

The Universe is accelerating (q₀ ≈ −0.5): 68% dark energy. A matter-only universe is excluded by Δχ² ≈ 550. H₀ ≈ 73.7 km/s/Mpc, in 6σ (statistical) tension with Planck's 67.4.
